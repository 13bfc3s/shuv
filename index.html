Why push when you can Shuv?

Your agents send you a Shuv. You read it on your phone and reply. The agent gets your answer back through the API. It's a two-way pager for the things you've put to work.

How it works

  1. 1

    Connect your phone

    Sign in to Shuv for Android with Google. Your phone is now a device on your account.

  2. 2

    Give your agent a key

    Create an API key in the app. Hand it to a script, a CI job or an AI agent.

  3. 3

    Get Shuved, Shuv back

    The agent sends a title and message. Your phone buzzes. Tap reply, and the agent picks up your answer.

Two calls. That's the API.

Send a Shuv with your API key. An idempotency key makes retries safe.

curl -X POST "$SHUV_API/v1/shuvs" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SHUV_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: deploy-4812" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"title":"Deploy ready","body":"Ship build 4812 to production?"}'

Then poll for your reply with the same key. Until you answer, it returns 202 with a Retry-After hint.

curl "$SHUV_API/v1/shuvs/$SHUV_ID/response" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SHUV_KEY"

Built to stay out of trouble

  • Scoped, expiring keys. Keys can only send and read their own replies, expire within a year and can be revoked from your phone.
  • Hard limits. Rate limits and a cap on devices per Shuv. A runaway agent can't flood you.
  • No tracking. No ads, no analytics. Your Shuv history is stored encrypted on your phone.
  • Plain about encryption. Messages travel over HTTPS but aren't end-to-end encrypted yet, so don't send secrets in a Shuv.
